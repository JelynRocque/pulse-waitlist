The Pulse accuracy standard

A tool that only works on some skin isn't accurate. It's lucky.

Skin scanners have a documented accuracy gap, and Stanford researchers found it's widest on deeper skin. Pulse closes it by rule. Each skin concern is tested on a consented panel of at least 30 people for each of ten skin tones, from Pearl to Onyx, labeled by board-certified dermatologists. If it's noticeably less accurate on any tone, more than 5 points behind the best, that concern stays out of the app. So whatever your skin looks like, you can trust what Pulse tells you.

The 5‑point rule

If it doesn't meet our bar, it doesn't ship.

If Pulse reads a skin concern correctly 90 times out of 100 on one skin tone, it has to get at least 85 right on every other tone. If it falls short, that feature waits until it doesn't.

Your face, your data

Privacy is built in from the first scan.

Face scans are biometric data. Pulse asks for your written consent before any scan and follows Illinois' biometric privacy law everywhere it operates.

  • Private by defaultScans and progress photos never appear on your profile.
  • Never soldYour scans are never sold, rented or shared with brands or advertisers.
  • Deletable anytimeDelete a scan or your account and it's gone within 30 days.
  • Model training is opt-inOnly adults who choose to help improve accuracy ever contribute images.
