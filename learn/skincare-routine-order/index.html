Skincare Routine Order: AM and PM, Step by Step

By Jelyn Andrews, Founder of Pulse · October 20, 2026

The Short Answer

Cleanse, then treatments like serums or prescriptions, then moisturizer. In the morning, sunscreen goes last before makeup. At night, take off sunscreen and makeup first, then use your treatment and moisturizer. As a rule, go from thinnest to thickest.

I'm a recovering product junkie. I have more face washes than I'd like to admit. And the thing that made the biggest difference wasn't a new product. It was using fewer of them, in the right order.

The One Rule

The order you apply products changes how well they work. The American Academy of Dermatology's version is simple:

  1. Cleanse with a gentle cleanser and pat dry.
  2. Apply any medication or treatment.
  3. Moisturizer and/or sunscreen.
  4. Makeup, if you wear it.

A good way to remember it: thinnest to thickest. Watery products go on before creams, so they can actually reach your skin.

Your Morning Routine

  1. Cleanser. Gentle. If your skin is dry, a rinse or a mild cleanser is plenty.
  2. Toner, if you use one. Optional. Skip it if it stings.
  3. Serum or treatment. Vitamin C is a common morning pick.
  4. Moisturizer.
  5. Sunscreen, last. Broad-spectrum, SPF 30 or higher. On deeper skin, a tinted one with iron oxide also helps keep dark spots from coming back.

Your Night Routine

  1. Take it all off. If you wore sunscreen or makeup, a cleansing balm or oil first breaks it down.
  2. Cleanser.
  3. Treatment. Night is when most people use retinoids and stronger actives.
  4. Moisturizer. Your night cream, or the same one you use in the morning. It doesn't need to be fancy.

Where Prescriptions Go

If a dermatologist prescribed something, it goes on right after cleansing, before anything else. Follow their directions over any general routine, including this one.

More Products Isn't a Better Routine

Piling on products, especially several "anti-aging" ones at once, can irritate your skin and make things look worse, not better. On medium and deep skin there's an extra cost: irritation can leave dark marks that take months to fade.

When my skin acts up, I strip it all the way back to a gentle cleanser and a moisturizer until it calms down. Then I add things back one at a time, so I know what's actually doing something.

Quick Questions

What order should I apply skincare in the morning?

Cleanser, then toner if you use one, then serum or treatment, then moisturizer, then sunscreen last before makeup.

What order should I apply skincare at night?

Remove sunscreen and makeup, cleanse, apply your treatment such as a retinoid, then moisturizer.

Where does prescription skincare go in a routine?

Right after cleansing, before other products, unless your dermatologist tells you otherwise.
